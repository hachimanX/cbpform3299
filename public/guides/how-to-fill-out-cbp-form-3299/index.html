How to Fill Out CBP Form 3299: Line-by-Line Instructions, Examples & 2026 Guide

By Customs Documentation Editorial Team · Reviewed by CBP Compliance Specialists · Updated October 2026

Shipping personal belongings, furniture, or household goods into the United States separate from your passenger flight requires formal border clearance. Under federal trade regulations (19 CFR § 148.6, 148.52, and 148.53), international ocean freight, air cargo, or express courier shipments cannot pass through a U.S. port of entry without CBP Form 3299: Declaration for Free Entry of Unaccompanied Articles.

Whether you are an American expat returning home, an immigrant moving on a permanent resident card, or a professional arriving on an H-1B, L-1, or O-1 work visa, Form 3299 informs customs officers whether your items enter duty-free or face import tariffs, merchandise processing fees, and mandatory inspections.

3. Returning Resident vs. Nonresident: The Legal Distinction

Your duty-free status hinges entirely on your declaration in Part II of Form 3299. U.S. Customs applies different legal standards based on your residency category:

Criteria Returning U.S. Resident (19 CFR § 148.31) Nonresident / Arriving Immigrant (19 CFR § 148.41)
Who Qualifies U.S. citizens and Green Card holders returning after residing abroad. Foreign nationals moving permanently, temporary visa holders (H-1B, L-1, F-1, J-1, O-1), and new lawful permanent residents arriving for the first time.
Personal Effects Duty-free if acquired in the U.S. or declared previously. Duty-free if owned prior to departure and intended for personal use.
Household Goods Exemption Must have been owned and used abroad for at least 1 full year (HTSUS 9804.00.05). Must have been owned and used abroad for at least 1 full year to claim duty-free entry under HTSUS 9804.00.05.
Required Supporting Proof Proof of overseas residence (lease, foreign utility bills, employment contracts). Valid U.S. visa, passport biographical page, I-94 arrival record, or Form I-797 approval notice.

4. The 1-Year Ownership Rule (HTSUS Heading 9804.00.05)

Under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (HTSUS Heading 9804.00.05), household goods—including tableware, furniture, libraries, artwork, and standard domestic equipment—qualify for zero customs duty only if:

  1. They were in your possession and used abroad for not less than one full year.
  2. They are not intended for any other person or for commercial sale.

5. Line-by-Line Field Instructions (Official Rev. 05/24)

Part I: Importer Information & Shipment Details

  • Box 1 (Name of Importer): Enter your full legal name exactly as printed in your passport. If your Bill of Lading displays a middle name, include it here.
  • Box 2 (Date of Birth): Format as MM/DD/YYYY.
  • Box 3 (Date of Arrival): The actual date you arrived (or plan to arrive) physically in the United States. This must match the entry stamp in your passport or your electronic I-94 record.
  • Box 4 (U.S. Address): Your residential delivery address in the U.S.
  • Box 5 (Foreign Address): Your previous residential street address abroad.
  • Box 6 (Reason for Moving): State your explicit reason (e.g., "Permanent relocation to the U.S.," "Returning U.S. citizen," or "Employment on H-1B visa").
  • Box 7 (U.S. Port of Arrival): The specific international airport or seaport where you personally cleared border control.
  • Box 8 (Name of Arriving Vessel/Carrier and Flight/Voyage #): The airline and flight number or ship name on which you traveled into the country. If your cargo traveled via courier (e.g., FedEx), note the courier Tracking/Air Waybill (AWB) number. (See our FedEx CBP Form 3299 Guide).
  • Box 9 (Residency Info): Provide your Social Security Number (SSN) if a U.S. citizen/resident, or passport number and issuing country if a foreign national.

Part II: Status of Arriving Person

  • Box A (Returning Resident): Check if you hold U.S. citizenship or permanent residency and are returning from living abroad. Provide dates of departure and return.
  • Box B (Nonresident): Check if you are entering on a work visa (H-1B, L-1, O-1), student visa (F-1), or permanent immigrant visa.

Part IV: Declaration of Articles

  • Section A (Exemption Claims): Check Box 1 for household effects used abroad ≥ 1 year (Tariff Item 9804.00.05). Check Box 2 for books and study libraries. Check Box 3 for professional tools of trade.
  • Section B (Declarations): Read items 1–10 carefully. Disclose all alcohol, tobacco, commercial goods, or articles acquired within the last 12 months. (See our Supplemental Declaration Guide).

Part VI: Declaration & Signature

Sign your legal signature and enter the date. Digital signatures generated through verified electronic signature tools are accepted by CBP brokers submitting through the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE).
