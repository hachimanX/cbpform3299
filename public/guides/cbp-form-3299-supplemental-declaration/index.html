CBP Form 3299 Supplemental Declaration: How to Itemize Household Goods & Regulated Articles

By Customs Documentation Editorial Team · Reviewed by CBP Compliance Specialists · Updated October 2026

Filing page one of CBP Form 3299 provides U.S. Customs with your personal identity and travel history. However, your shipment's legal entry is decided on Page 2: Part IV (Section D) and the Supplemental Declaration of Articles.

Under 19 CFR § 148.52 and § 148.53, international shipments of unaccompanied personal effects and household goods cannot clear customs using generic descriptions such as "Miscellaneous Household Goods" or "Packed by Owner (PBO)." Customs brokers and port officers flag vague manifests as high-risk contraband indicators, triggering mandatory physical examinations (CET / VACIS exams) that cost between $500 and $2,000+ in non-reimbursable inspection fees.

A compliant Supplemental Declaration requires four distinct categories of data:

4. Declaring Alcohol & Wine Cellars (Part IV-B, Line 2)

Importing wine, beer, or distilled spirits within your household shipment requires strict compliance with federal and state regulations:

  • Federal Excise Taxes & CBP Duties: All personal alcohol shipments are subject to federal excise tax (roughly $0.21 to $0.67 per 750ml bottle of wine, and up to $2.14+ per bottle for 80-proof spirits), plus customs duty.
  • State Liquor Control Boards (ABC): Federal customs enforces the liquor regulations of the state where the shipment is physically delivered. States like California permit reasonable personal collections, while states like Utah or Pennsylvania enforce rigid gallonage ceilings or require state import permits.
  • Itemization Mandate: You must attach a bottle-by-bottle list stating the brand, volume in liters, and alcohol by volume percentage (ABV%).

5. Firearms & Ammunition Declarations (27 CFR Part 478)

Ammunition and firearm declarations on CBP Form 3299 represent the highest-risk compliance category in international relocation. Under federal law (27 CFR Part 478 and the Arms Export Control Act), transporting firearms and ammunition into the United States is strictly regulated by the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF) in conjunction with CBP.

  • Disclose in Part IV, Section B: Check YES for firearms and munitions.
  • Itemize on Page 2: State the precise round count, caliber (e.g., 500 rounds 9x19mm Parabellum, 200 rounds .308 Win), and value.
  • Mandatory Documentation: Attach your approved ATF Form 6 (Part I). Filing Form 3299 without an ATF permit will result in immediate weapon confiscation, port impoundment, and potential criminal referral.
