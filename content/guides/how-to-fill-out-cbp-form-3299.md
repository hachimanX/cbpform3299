Shipping personal belongings, furniture, or household goods into the United States separate from your passenger flight requires formal border clearance. Under federal trade regulations (**19 CFR § 148.6, 148.52, and 148.53**), international ocean freight, air cargo, or courier shipments cannot pass through a U.S. port of entry without **CBP Form 3299: Declaration for Free Entry of Unaccompanied Articles**.

Whether you are an American expat returning home, an immigrant moving on a permanent visa, or a foreign national arriving on an H-1B, L-1, or O-1 work visa, Form 3299 informs customs officers whether your items enter duty-free or face import duties, merchandise processing fees, and mandatory inspections.

```
 │ 1. IDENTITY & TRAVEL VERIFICATION                                           │
 │ • Legal name exactly as printed in your passport                            │
 │ • Date of birth, Social Security Number (or foreign passport number)       │
 │ • Exact U.S. arrival date and international port of entry (airport/seaport) │
 │ • Arriving flight number or vessel name                                     │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 2. ADDRESSES & CARRIER SHIPMENT DATA                                        │
 │ • Foreign street address where you lived abroad                             │
 │ • U.S. residential delivery address where items will arrive                 │
 │ • Shipping carrier details: Ocean Bill of Lading (OBL), Air Waybill (AWB),  │
 │   or courier tracking number (e.g., FedEx AWB #)                            │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 3. RESIDENCY STATUS & VISA CLASSIFICATION                                   │
 │ • Status designation: Returning U.S. Resident vs. Nonresident               │

 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤

 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

If any of these four pillars is inconsistent—such as an arrival date that doesn't match your official I-94 travel record, or a delivery address that differs from your bill of lading—customs will place an administrative hold on your shipment.

## 4. Returning Resident vs. Nonresident: The Legal Distinction

Your duty-free status hinges entirely on your declaration in **Part II** of Form 3299. U.S. Customs applies different legal standards based on your residency category:

| Criteria | Returning U.S. Resident (19 CFR § 148.31) | Nonresident / Arriving Immigrant (19 CFR § 148.41) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Who Qualifies** | U.S. citizens and Green Card holders returning after residing abroad. | Foreign nationals moving permanently, temporary visa holders (H-1B, L-1, F-1, J-1, O-1), and new lawful permanent residents arriving for the first time. |
| **Personal Effects** | Duty-free if acquired in the U.S. or declared previously. | Duty-free if owned prior to departure and intended for personal use. |
| **Household Goods Exemption** | Must have been owned and used abroad for **at least 1 full year** (HTSUS 9804.00.05). | Must have been owned and used abroad for **at least 1 full year** to claim duty-free entry under HTSUS 9804.00.05. |
| **Required Supporting Proof** | Proof of overseas residence (lease, foreign utility bills, employment contracts). | Valid U.S. visa, passport biographical page, I-94 arrival record, or Form I-797 approval notice. |

> **Key Rule (19 CFR § 148.52):** Articles must arrive in the United States within **one year** of your own arrival date to qualify for free entry under your personal declaration. Shipments arriving after one year require an explicit customs extension waiver.

## 5. The 1-Year Ownership Rule (HTSUS Heading 9804.00.05)

Under the Harmonized Tariff Schedule of the United States (**HTSUS Heading 9804.00.05**), household goods—including tableware, furniture, libraries, artwork, and standard domestic equipment—qualify for zero customs duty **only if**:

1. They were in your possession and used abroad for **not less than one full year**.
2. They are not intended for any other person or for commercial sale.

```
                   1-YEAR HOUSEHOLD GOODS TEST (19 CFR § 148.52)
                   
          Item owned & used abroad for ≥ 12 continuous months?
                                │
               ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
               ▼                                 ▼
             YES                                NO
       ┌──────────────┐                  ┌──────────────┐
       │  DUTY-FREE   │                  │   TAXABLE    │
       │ Check Box 1  │                  │ Itemize in   │
       │ in Part IV-A │                  │ Part IV-D &  │
       │ (9804.00.05) │                  │ pay duty/fee │
       └──────────────┘                  └──────────────┘
```

If you purchased brand-new furniture or high-end electronics within months of your move, those items **do not qualify** for the 1-year duty-free exemption. You must declare them separately in Part IV, Section D, where CBP calculates duty based on their purchase price.

## 6. Line-by-Line Field Instructions (Official Rev. 05/24)

Always use the official, current **CBP Form 3299 (Rev. 05/24)**. Older revisions may trigger immediate rejection at customs entry processing.

### Part I: Importer Information & Shipment Details

* **Box 1 (Name of Importer):** Enter your full legal name exactly as printed in your passport. If your Bill of Lading displays a middle name, include it here.
* **Box 2 (Date of Birth):** Format as `MM/DD/YYYY`.
* **Box 3 (Date of Arrival):** The actual date you arrived (or plan to arrive) physically in the United States. This must match the entry stamp in your passport or your electronic I-94 record.
* **Box 4 (U.S. Address):** Your residential delivery address in the U.S. If you have not finalized a permanent home, provide your corporate address, temporary rental, or hotel where you can receive certified postal notices.
* **Box 5 (Foreign Address):** Your previous residential street address abroad.
* **Box 6 (Reason for Moving):** State your explicit reason (e.g., *"Permanent relocation to the U.S.," "End of foreign employment assignment," "Returning U.S. citizen,"* or *"Employment on H-1B visa"*).
* **Box 7 (U.S. Port of Arrival):** The specific international airport or seaport where you personally cleared border control.
* **Box 8 (Name of Arriving Vessel/Carrier and Flight/Voyage #):** The airline and flight number or ship name on which you traveled into the country. If your cargo traveled via courier (e.g., FedEx), note the courier Tracking/Air Waybill (AWB) number. *(See our dedicated [FedEx CBP Form 3299 Guide](/guides/cbp-form-3299-fedex) for express courier details).*
* **Box 9 (Residency Info):** Provide your social security number (SSN) if a U.S. citizen or green card holder, or passport number and issuing country if a foreign national.
* **Box 10 (Members of Family in Shipment):** List accompanying dependents (spouse, children) whose personal effects share the shipping container.

### Part II: Status of Arriving Person

Mark the single checkbox that reflects your legal standing:

* **Box A (Returning Resident of the U.S.):** Check if you hold U.S. citizenship or permanent residency and are returning from living abroad. You must provide the date you left the U.S. and your departure date for return.
* **Box B (Nonresident):** Check if you are emigrating permanently, or entering on a non-immigrant work/student visa (H-1B, L-1, F-1, O-1). State your foreign citizenship and U.S. visa classification.

### Part III: Military Personnel & Evacuees Only

* **Boxes 11–13:** **Leave completely blank** unless you are an active-duty member of the U.S. Armed Forces, a Department of Defense civilian employee, or an official government evacuee traveling under military travel orders.

### Part IV: Declaration of Articles

Part IV determines whether duties are owed. It is split into two sections across Pages 1 and 2:

#### Section A (Exemption Claims)
* Check **Box 1** if claiming free entry under Tariff Item **9804.00.05** for household goods owned and used abroad for 1 year or more.
* Check **Box 2** if claiming free entry for books, libraries, and study equipment used abroad.
* Check **Box 3** for professional tools of trade (instruments, specialized machinery) owned and used in your profession overseas.

#### Section B (Specific Declarations)
Read items 1 through 10 carefully and check the appropriate boxes. You must disclose:
* **Alcoholic beverages and tobacco products:** Must be itemized on Page 2 with bottle count, volume (liters), and alcohol-by-volume percentage (ABV%). *(See our comprehensive [Supplemental Declaration & Alcohol Guide](/guides/cbp-form-3299-supplemental-declaration)).*
* **Articles acquired abroad under 1 year of age:** Must be declared at fair market value.
* **Commercial goods or samples:** Items intended for business resale.

### Part V: Carrier / Broker Certification

* This section is completed by your freight forwarder or licensed customs broker (CHB). Leave blank if you are self-filing or providing the document to your mover.

### Part VI: Declaration & Certification

* **Signature & Date:** Sign your legal signature and record the date. Digital signatures generated through verified electronic signature tools are accepted by CBP brokers submitting through the Automated Commercial Environment (ACE).

## 7. Required Companion Documents for Customs Clearance

Filing Form 3299 alone is not sufficient. Your licensed customs broker must bundle Form 3299 into an entry packet containing:

1. **Copy of Passport Biographical Page:** Demonstrates legal identity.
2. **U.S. Visa or ESTA / Permanent Resident Card:** Proves legal entry status.
3. **CBP Electronic I-94 Arrival Record:** Downloadable from the official [CBP I-94 Portal](https://i94.cbp.dhs.gov) once you land in the U.S.
4. **Ocean Bill of Lading (OBL) or Air Waybill (AWB):** Provided by your shipping line or air carrier.
5. **Itemized Customs Packing List & Inventory:** A piece-by-piece list detailing carton numbers, contents, and estimated replacement values. *(Auto-generated when using [cbpform3299.com](https://cbpform3299.com)).*

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What happens if my shipment arrives before I do?
If your freight arrives at a U.S. seaport before you land physically in the country, customs cannot process an unaccompanied duty-free entry under your name. The container must remain in bonded storage (Container Freight Station / CFS) under warehouse bond until your passport and I-94 record are generated upon arrival.

### Do I have to pay duty on computers and electronics?
Laptops, tablets, and consumer electronics owned and used abroad for over one year qualify for free entry under household goods. If purchased new within the past year, they must be declared in Part IV-D. Most personal computing equipment carries 0% duty under the Information Technology Agreement (ITA), but failing to declare them can incur penalties.
