# CBP Form 3299 Supplemental Declaration: How to Itemize Household Goods, Alcohol, and Regulated Articles

**By Customs Documentation Editorial Team** · Reviewed by CBP Compliance Specialists · Updated for 2026  
**Tags:** CBP Form 3299 Supplemental Declaration, List of Articles, Ammunition Declaration, Customs Inventory, 19 CFR 148 · **Category:** US Customs Compliance Guides

---

Filing page one of **CBP Form 3299** provides U.S. Customs with your personal identity and travel history. However, your shipment's legal entry is decided on **Page 2: Part IV (Section D) and the Supplemental Declaration of Articles**.

Under **19 CFR § 148.52 and § 148.53**, international shipments of unaccompanied personal effects and household goods cannot clear customs using generic descriptions such as *"Miscellaneous Household Goods"* or *"Packed by Owner (PBO)."* Customs brokers and port officers flag vague manifests as high-risk contraband indicators, triggering mandatory physical examinations (CET / VACIS exams) that cost between **$500 and $2,000+** in non-reimbursable inspection fees.

## 1. What Information Do You Need to Include?

A compliant Supplemental Declaration requires four distinct categories of data:

```
                  SUPPLEMENTAL DECLARATION DATA STRUCTURE
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ 1. CARTON & PACKAGE NUMBERING                                               │
 │ • Sequential numbering of all shipping boxes (Carton 01, Carton 02, etc.)   │
 │ • Concrete item descriptions (e.g., "18 used cotton shirts", not "clothes") │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 2. VALUATION & OWNERSHIP TIMELINES                                          │
 │ • Depreciated fair market replacement value in USD for used personal items  │
 │ • Explicit segregation of goods owned ≥ 1 year vs. items owned < 1 year     │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 3. REGULATED COMMODITIES (ALCOHOL & TOBACCO)                                │
 │ • Bottle counts, total volume in liters, and Alcohol by Volume percentage   │
 │ • Compliance with destination state Alcoholic Beverage Control (ABC) rules │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ 4. RESTRICTED COMMODITIES (AMMUNITION & FIREARMS)                           │
 │ • Caliber, manufacturer, and round count for ammunition                     │
 │ • Pre-approved ATF Form 6 (Form 5330.3A) import authorization number        │
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

## 5. Declaring Alcohol & Wine Cellars (Part IV-B, Line 2)

Importing wine, beer, or distilled spirits within your household shipment requires strict compliance with federal and state regulations:

* **Federal Excise Taxes & CBP Duties:** All personal alcohol shipments are subject to federal excise tax (roughly $0.21 to $0.67 per 750ml bottle of wine, and up to $2.14+ per bottle for 80-proof spirits), plus customs duty.
* **State Liquor Control Boards (ABC):** Federal customs enforces the liquor regulations of the **state where the shipment is physically delivered**. States like California permit reasonable personal collections, while states like Utah or Pennsylvania enforce rigid gallonage ceilings or require state import permits.
* **Itemization Mandate:** You must attach a bottle-by-bottle list stating the brand, volume in liters, and alcohol by volume percentage (ABV%).

## 6. Firearms & Ammunition Declarations (27 CFR Part 478)

Ammunition and firearm declarations on CBP Form 3299 represent the highest-risk compliance category in international relocation. 

Under federal law (**27 CFR Part 478 and the Arms Export Control Act**), transporting firearms and ammunition into the United States is strictly regulated by the **Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives (ATF)** in conjunction with CBP.

```
                    FIREARMS & AMMUNITION REQUIREMENTS
 ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
 │ U.S. CITIZENS / RETURNING RESIDENTS                                         │
 │ • Firearms acquired in the U.S. and taken abroad: Exempt from ATF Form 6 if │
 │   proven via CBP Form 4457 (Certificate of Registration for Personal Effects)│
 │ • Firearms/ammunition acquired abroad: Mandatory approved ATF Form 6        │
 │   (Form 5330.3A) from the ATF prior to shipping.                            │
 ├─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
 │ NONRESIDENTS / VISITING ALIENS / IMMIGRANTS                                 │
 │ • MUST obtain an approved ATF Form 6 import permit issued by ATF.           │
 │ • Nonimmigrant visa holders (H-1B, L-1, F-1) must possess a valid state     │
 │   hunting license or an official ATF waiver exception.                      │
 │ • Ammunition must be declared with exact caliber, manufacturer, round count.│
 └─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Ammunition Declaration Protocol on Form 3299:
1. **Disclose in Part IV, Section B:** Check **YES** for firearms and munitions.
2. **Itemize on Page 2:** State the precise round count, caliber (e.g., *500 rounds 9x19mm Parabellum, 200 rounds .308 Win*), and value.
3. **Carrier Hazmat Compliance:** Ocean carriers and air freight forwarders enforce rigid International Maritime Dangerous Goods (IMDG) or IATA rules. Ammunition must be packed separately from firearms in approved consumer commodity packages (UN 0012). **Express couriers like FedEx do not accept consumer ammunition in unaccompanied luggage.**
4. **Mandatory Documentation:** Attach your approved **ATF Form 6 (Part I)**. Filing Form 3299 without an ATF permit will result in immediate weapon confiscation, port impoundment, and potential criminal referral.
